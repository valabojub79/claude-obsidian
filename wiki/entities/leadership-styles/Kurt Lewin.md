# Kurt Lewin

German-American social psychologist, widely regarded as a founder of social, organizational, and applied psychology. In the context of this wiki he is the origin of the modern study of leadership styles.

## Contribution

With colleagues Lippitt and White, Lewin's 1939 study (*Experiments in social space*, Harvard Educational Review) identified three leadership styles by observing groups under each: autocratic, democratic, and laissez-faire. This is the foundational typology that later frameworks extend. See [[Lewin's Leadership Styles]].

## Significance

[[leadership-styles-simply-psychology]] credits Lewin's 1939 research as the basis for modern leadership theory. [[leadership-styles-managing-life-at-work]] places the Lewin/Lippitt/White typology as the canonical "decision-making" category of leadership styles. [[leadership-styles-muse]] lists him among the researchers grounding its catalog, alongside [[Bernard M. Bass]] and [[Robert K. Greenleaf]].
