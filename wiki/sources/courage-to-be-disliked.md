# The Courage to be Disliked (Hinglish)

A Hinglish (Hindi + English) adaptation of the Japanese bestseller by [[Fumitake Koga]] and [[Ichiro Kishimi]], introducing [[Adlerian Psychology]] through a Socratic dialogue format. Originally published in Japanese (2013); one of the most widely-read books on Adlerian thought.

## Format

Structured as five nights of conversation between two unnamed characters: a discontented YOUTH who believes the world and his past have made him who he is, and a PHILOSOPHER who challenges every assumption using [[Alfred Adler]]'s framework. The Hinglish version preserves the full content while making it accessible to Hindi-English bilingual readers.

## The Five Nights

### Night 1: Deny Trauma

Introduces [[Alfred Adler]] as the "unknown third giant" alongside Freud and Jung. Adler's psychology is contrasted with Freudian etiology. The central provocation: trauma does not determine behavior. People use past events teleologically — to justify where they are now and avoid changing. Emotions including anger are purposively fabricated tools, not reactions. Unhappiness is chosen. "Lifestyle" (one's personality and worldview) is self-selected, not imposed.

### Night 2: All Problems Are Interpersonal Relationship Problems

All human problems reduce to interpersonal relationships. Feelings of [[Inferiority Feelings|inferiority]] are universal and can be healthy motivators; they become pathological when weaponized as an inferiority complex or superiority complex (bragging). Life is not a competition. Vertical relationships (hierarchical, competitive) cause suffering; horizontal relationships (equal, cooperative) enable growth. Power struggles escalate to revenge cycles when one party refuses to lose. The "life-lie" (seikatsu-uso) is the set of excuses people manufacture to avoid facing their life tasks.

### Night 3: Discard Other People's Tasks

The desire for recognition is a trap: living to satisfy others' expectations removes freedom. Adler's concept of "separation of tasks" clarifies whose responsibility any given situation is. You are only responsible for your own tasks; entering other people's tasks causes conflict. True freedom is accepting that some people will dislike you as a consequence of living on your own terms. You hold the cards to every interpersonal relationship because you always choose how to respond.

### Night 4: Where the Center of the World Is

The goal of all interpersonal relationships is Gemeinschaftsgefuhl (community feeling / social interest) — a sense of belonging to a larger whole. "You are not the center of the world" counters the narcissistic framing. Rebuke and praise are both vertical-relationship tools that create dependency. The Adlerian alternative is encouragement: horizontal support for effort and contribution. Feeling of value arises not from being special but from "I am useful to someone." The smallest unit of community is "I and you"; it expands outward without limit.

### Night 5: To Live in Earnest in the Here and Now

Self-acceptance (accepting the unchangeable self) is distinct from self-affirmation (convincing yourself you're great). Trust (shinrai) differs from confidence (shinyou): trust is unconditional, confidence is conditional. The essence of work is contribution to the common good, not self-interest. "The courage to be normal" is harder than the desire to be special. Life is not a linear journey toward a destination — it is a series of moments, each complete. Living teleologically toward a far-off goal means never living now (the greatest life-lie). The meaning you assign to life is yours alone to create.

## Key Adlerian Claims

| Claim | Conventional View | Adlerian View |
|---|---|---|
| Why we are who we are | Past (etiology) | Goals we hold now (teleology) |
| Can people change? | Hard without understanding causes | Yes, at any moment |
| Source of all problems | Neurosis, environment | Interpersonal relationships |
| Purpose of emotions | Reactions to stimuli | Tools fabricated toward goals |
| What happiness requires | Approval, success | Community feeling + courage |
| What freedom costs | Nothing | Being disliked |
