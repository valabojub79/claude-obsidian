# Servant Leadership

The leader prioritizes others' needs above their own and focuses on elevating team members to reach their full potential ([[leadership-styles-muse]], [[leadership-styles-imd]]). The term originates with [[Robert K. Greenleaf]].

## The seven dimensions

[[leadership-styles-managing-life-at-work]] (Liden et al. 2008/2015) gives a multidimensional model: emotional healing, community contribution, conceptual skills, empowering, supporting growth, prioritizing followers, and ethical behavior.

## Pros

- Boosts morale, trust, performance, and culture; builds loyalty.
- Improves communication across hierarchies; ethical decision foundation; prevents conflict.

## Cons

- Challenging to consistently deprioritize one's own needs ([[leadership-styles-muse]]).
- Can be slow where decisive direction is needed.

## Best used when

Building trust with new teams and supporting teams through challenges ([[leadership-styles-muse]], [[determine-your-leadership-style-harvard]]).

> [!key-insight] The standout empirical claim
> [[leadership-styles-managing-life-at-work]] calls servant leadership "one of the most promising leadership styles," reporting it exceeds [[Transformational Leadership]] and ethical leadership in effectiveness across multiple outcomes. It satisfies followers' psychological needs and seeds a self-reinforcing "serving culture."
