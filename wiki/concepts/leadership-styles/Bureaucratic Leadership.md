# Bureaucratic Leadership

Leaders follow prescribed rules, systems, and a clear chain of command; authority derives from formal position rather than personal traits ([[leadership-styles-muse]], [[determine-your-leadership-style-harvard]]). Emphasizes rationality and avoidance of ambiguity ([[leadership-styles-simply-psychology]]).

## Pros

- Stability through a systematized approach; scalable and predictable.
- Creates best practices; provides job security; centralizes roles.

## Cons

- Inflexible; ignores employee creativity.
- Limits advancement, can stifle productivity, and resists change during organizational shifts.

## Best used when

Strict compliance requirements, or routine and predictable processes ([[leadership-styles-muse]]).

## Notes

Distinct from [[Autocratic Leadership]]: bureaucratic authority is impersonal (the rules rule), whereas autocratic authority is personal (the leader decides). The two often co-occur in highly structured environments.
