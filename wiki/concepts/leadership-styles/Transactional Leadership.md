# Transactional Leadership

Treats the leader-follower relationship as an exchange: followers receive rewards (pay, bonuses) for meeting performance criteria and penalties for missing them ([[leadership-styles-imd]], [[leadership-styles-simply-psychology]]). Studied extensively by [[Bernard M. Bass]] as the counterpart to [[Transformational Leadership]].

## Mechanisms

[[leadership-styles-managing-life-at-work]] decomposes it into **contingent reward** and **management by exception** (active and passive). Contingent rewards reliably boost performance; exception management shows mixed results.

## Pros

- Clear expectations; eliminates confusion and guesswork.
- Efficient task completion; motivation through explicit incentives.

## Cons

- Diminishing returns as employees become purely transactional.
- Requires incentives consistently aligned with company goals.
- Does not foster intrinsic motivation or innovation.

## Best used when

Clear, routine tasks where the team is motivated by explicit directions and rewards ([[leadership-styles-muse]]).

## Evidence

- [[leadership-styles-simply-psychology]] (Kahai et al. 2004): group efficacy was higher under the transactional condition in one experiment.
- But MacKenzie et al. (2001): transformational behaviors relate more strongly to sales performance than transactional, contrary to sales-industry intuition. Pairs with [[Transformational Leadership]].
