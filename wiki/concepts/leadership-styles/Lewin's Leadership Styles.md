# Lewin's Leadership Styles

The foundational typology of leadership styles, from [[Kurt Lewin]] and colleagues Lippitt and White (1939, *Experiments in social space*). It is the base from which nearly every later taxonomy in [[Leadership Style]] descends ([[leadership-styles-simply-psychology]], [[leadership-styles-managing-life-at-work]]).

## The three styles

1. **[[Autocratic Leadership]]** (authoritarian): the leader makes decisions alone, with a clear division between leader and members.
2. **[[Democratic Leadership]]** (participative): the leader guides but encourages participation and shares responsibility.
3. **[[Laissez-Faire Leadership]]** (delegative): the leader sets the vision and hands implementation to the team.

## Why it endures

Lewin's study was empirical (observing groups of schoolchildren under each style) rather than purely prescriptive, which is why later academic work ([[leadership-styles-managing-life-at-work]] groups these as the "decision-making" category) treats it as the canonical decision-making axis. The 1939 findings generally favored the democratic style for morale and quality, though autocratic produced more output under close supervision.

## Lineage

Later styles ([[Transformational Leadership]], [[Servant Leadership]], [[Transactional Leadership]], etc.) extend beyond Lewin's decision-making axis into motivation, values, and adaptation. See [[Leadership Style]] for the full map.
