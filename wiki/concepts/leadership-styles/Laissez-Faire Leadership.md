# Laissez-Faire Leadership

Also called **delegative** ([[leadership-styles-imd]]). One of [[Lewin's Leadership Styles]]. A hands-off approach: the leader provides tools, resources, and vision, then lets the team make its own decisions ([[leadership-styles-muse]], [[leadership-styles-simply-psychology]]).

## Pros

- Empowering for creative, self-motivated teams; leverages member experience.
- Builds personal responsibility and supports fast problem-solving.
- [[leadership-styles-simply-psychology]]: associated with higher organizational retention.

## Cons

- Chaos and confusion with unorganized teams; unclear accountability.
- Hard on newcomers who need direction ([[leadership-styles-imd]]).
- Requires highly skilled members to work at all.

> [!contradiction] How harmful is it?
> [[leadership-styles-managing-life-at-work]] (academic) calls laissez-faire "severely detrimental" to effectiveness and follower well-being, treating leader inaction as a real failure. The practitioner sources ([[leadership-styles-muse]], [[leadership-styles-imd]], [[determine-your-leadership-style-harvard]]) frame it positively as empowering for the right team. The gap is the team: it helps expert, self-directed teams and harms dependent ones.

## Best used when

Expert, accountable, organized, self-directed teams where autonomy is the goal ([[leadership-styles-muse]], [[leadership-styles-simply-psychology]]).
