# Community Feeling

German: *Gemeinschaftsgefuhl*. The sense of belonging to — and contributing to — a community larger than oneself. In [[Adlerian Psychology]], community feeling is the goal of all interpersonal development and the definition of mental health. Without it, all three of Adler's life tasks (work, friendship, love) break down.

## What It Is

Community feeling is not membership in a group. It is a felt orientation: "I belong here. I can contribute here. I am not alone." It encompasses:

- Feeling useful to at least one other person
- Perceiving the world as a safe enough place to contribute
- Identifying with something beyond one's immediate self-interest

Adler placed the smallest unit of community at "I and you" — any genuine two-person relationship. From there, it expands outward: family, neighborhood, society, humanity, the cosmos.

## Why It Produces Happiness

Adler's claim: happiness = community feeling. The mechanism is contribution. When you feel that your existence makes a difference to someone else — not because you are special, but because you are here and participating — you experience your own worth.

This bypasses the approval trap. You do not need to be praised to feel valuable. You need to feel useful. "I am of use to someone" is sufficient. The moment you can say this genuinely, unhappiness dissolves.

## The Encouragement Approach

The opposite of community feeling is self-focus: "What do I get? How am I seen? Am I winning?" This produces vertical relationships — competition, comparison, praise-seeking, rebuke-fearing.

Adler's alternative to rebuke and praise is **encouragement**: horizontal support for a person's effort and contribution, without creating the dependency that comes from reward/punishment cycles.

- Praise says: "You did well. (Implicitly: I am the judge.)"
- Encouragement says: "You worked hard on this. (Implicitly: I see your effort.)"

Praise is vertical (evaluator to evaluated). Encouragement is horizontal (equal to equal).

## You Are Not the Center

Community feeling requires acknowledging that you are not the center of the world. The world does not revolve around your needs, preferences, or anxieties. This is not self-erasure — it is correct calibration. You are one participant among many participants, each with equal standing.

Excessive self-consciousness (over-focus on how others perceive you) is the enemy of community feeling. It keeps you inside your own head rather than contributing outward.

## Scale

Adler's community feeling is not bounded. The community that Adler points toward is, in the extreme, "all of humanity" and beyond — the universe as community. The practical implication: if one community (workplace, family) is toxic, don't conclude you don't belong anywhere. Expand the frame. "I am part of something larger."

## Connections

Community feeling is built through [[Separation of Tasks]] (stop trying to control others) and horizontal relationships (stop competing). It is blocked by the desire for recognition (needing approval) and by excessive self-focus. See [[Adlerian Psychology]] for the full framework.
