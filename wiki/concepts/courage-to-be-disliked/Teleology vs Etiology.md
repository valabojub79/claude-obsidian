# Teleology vs Etiology

The central methodological divide in [[Adlerian Psychology]]. Etiology explains behavior by its causes (the past); teleology explains it by its purposes (the future). Adler chose teleology. Freud chose etiology. This single choice produces radically different pictures of human freedom, change, and responsibility.

## Etiology (Freudian / Causal View)

- Behavior is caused by prior events: childhood trauma, repressed memories, instinctual drives.
- The past determines the present. Understanding the cause is the path to change.
- Change is hard because causes are buried in the unconscious.
- Logical conclusion: you are not fully responsible for who you are.

## Teleology (Adlerian / Purpose View)

- Behavior is explained by the goal it serves right now: what is this person trying to achieve?
- The past does not determine the present. People selectively use past experiences to justify current choices.
- Change is possible at any moment because the goal can change at any moment.
- Logical conclusion: you are responsible for who you are.

## The Anger Example

Etiology: "I got angry because he insulted me." (Past event caused emotion.)

Teleology: "I fabricated anger to force him into submission." (Emotion was a tool toward a goal — getting my way, winning the argument.)

Adler's view: emotions are not reactions, they are instruments. You reach into your emotional toolkit and pull out the one that serves your current purpose.

## The Trauma Example

Etiology: "My difficult childhood caused my current unhappiness."

Teleology: "I am using memories of my difficult childhood to avoid the risk of changing now."

This is not callousness. Adler accepts that bad things happen. The claim is specifically that bad things do not automatically produce bad outcomes — the person's interpretation and chosen response determine the outcome. The past is inert; only the meaning assigned to it has power.

## Why This Matters

If etiology is true, human freedom is limited and therapy requires excavating the past. If teleology is true, change is available right now and the relevant question is always: what goal am I currently serving with this behavior?

The courage to be disliked rests on teleology: if you are unhappy, it is because you are currently choosing a lifestyle that produces unhappiness. That lifestyle can be changed.

## Connections

This is the foundational concept of [[Adlerian Psychology]]. All other Adlerian ideas — [[Separation of Tasks]], [[Community Feeling]], [[Inferiority Feelings]] — build on the teleological assumption.
