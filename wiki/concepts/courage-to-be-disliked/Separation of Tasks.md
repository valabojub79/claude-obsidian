# Separation of Tasks

An [[Adlerian Psychology|Adlerian]] framework for resolving interpersonal friction by clarifying whose responsibility any given situation belongs to. The core question: "Whose task is this?"

## The Principle

Every problem, decision, or burden belongs to one person's "task" to resolve. You can identify whose task something is by asking: who ultimately has to live with the consequences?

- A child's study habits: the child's task (the child lives with the results of learning or not learning).
- Your boss's opinion of you: the boss's task (what others think of you is their business, not yours).
- Whether others like you: their task (you cannot control others' feelings).
- How you respond to others: your task (you hold all the cards here).

## Freedom Through Separation

Most interpersonal suffering, in Adler's view, comes from one of two errors:

1. **Entering other people's tasks** — trying to control, fix, or manage what belongs to someone else. This creates conflict because it is experienced as intrusion.
2. **Letting others enter your tasks** — allowing others' opinions, judgments, and expectations to govern your choices. This produces the need for recognition, which makes you unfree.

Separation of tasks does not mean indifference. You can care about someone without taking on their task. You can offer help without imposing it. The red string metaphor: a relationship can be close without being a rigid chain of obligation and control.

## The Desire for Recognition

The desire for approval from others is the mechanism by which people surrender their tasks. "I need others to approve of my choices" = I have handed my task to someone else.

Adler is blunt: do not live to satisfy the expectations of others. This is not anti-social — it is pre-social. You cannot contribute meaningfully to a community while living as a mirror of others' expectations.

## Practical Application

Before feeling responsible for someone else's emotional state, ask: whose task is it to manage that emotion? Before seeking approval for a decision, ask: who lives with the consequences of this decision?

The answer clarifies the boundary.

## Connections

Separation of tasks enables the freedom that makes [[Community Feeling]] possible. Without it, relationships become power struggles. With it, horizontal (equal) relationships become possible. See [[Adlerian Psychology]] for the broader framework.
