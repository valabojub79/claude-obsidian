# Leadership Styles: Definition, Types, and Examples

- Publication: Managing Life at Work (academically grounded, cites primary research)
- Author: not named

## Main thesis

Leadership styles are the behavior patterns leaders adopt, made of complementary behaviors that co-occur frequently. Organized into four types so leaders can select approaches matching situational demands. "Leadership styles have stronger effects on effectiveness than leaders' traits and skills." Servant leadership shows superior outcomes.

## Four core types

### 1. Decision-Making styles
- Participative - followers have active role (consultation, joint decisions, delegation); increases engagement, commitment, creativity.
- Autocratic - asserts authority, expects obedience; unilateral decisions; effective with hierarchy-respecting followers; risks dissatisfaction and stress.
- Laissez-Faire - avoids decisions, action, responsibility; "severely detrimental" to effectiveness and well-being.

### 2. Everyday styles
- Consideration - people-oriented; concern, conflict resolution, respect, appreciation; strong on job satisfaction.
- Initiating Structure - task-oriented; organizing work, deadlines, monitoring; enhances task performance.
- Transactional - contingent reward + active/passive management by exception; contingent rewards boost performance.

### 3. Beyond-everyday styles
- Visionary - communicates image of future; clarifies direction, purpose; boosts innovation, change readiness.
- Transformational - inspires followers to transcend self-interest. Four dimensions: idealized influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualized consideration.
- Empowering - shares power; supports autonomy and self-reliance; effective for innovation/creativity.

### 4. Values-Based styles
- Ethical - fair, honest, promotes follower ethical conduct; prevents unethical behavior and turnover.
- Authentic - acts in keeping with self, purpose, values. Aspects: personal, relational, developmental. Behaviors: relational transparency, self-awareness, unbiased processing, internalized moral perspective.
- Servant - oriented to serving others; seven dimensions (emotional healing, community contribution, conceptual skills, empowering, supporting growth, prioritizing followers, ethical behavior). "One of the most promising leadership styles."

## Researchers referenced

- Lewin, Lippitt, White (1939) - participative/autocratic/laissez-faire typology
- Fleishman (1953) - consideration and initiating structure
- Bass & Riggio (2006) - transformational leadership
- Brown, Trevino, Harrison (2005) - ethical leadership
- Walumbwa et al. (2008) - authentic leadership
- Liden et al. (2008, 2015) - servant leadership

## Key distinctions

Theoretical vs practical (leaders blend styles); automatic vs monitored (habitual default vs deliberate analysis); broader styles encompass narrower ones.
