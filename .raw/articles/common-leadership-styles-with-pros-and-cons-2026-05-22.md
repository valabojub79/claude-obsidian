# 10 Most Common Leadership Styles and Their Pros and Cons (2024)

- Author: Kat Boogaard
- Publication: The Muse
- Updated: 2024-12-02
- Experts quoted: Joyel Crawford (career coach, leadership development consultant); Tara Padua (executive coach, entrepreneur, startup advisor)
- Theoretical grounding: cites leadership researchers Kurt Lewin, Bernard M. Bass, and Robert K. Greenleaf.

## Main Thesis

No single "perfect" leadership style exists. Effectiveness depends on context, and leaders can adapt their approach. Coaching, servant, situational, and transformational styles are increasingly valued as employees seek flexibility and growth. "Your leadership style isn't an annual membership" — adjustments are achievable through habit replacement and practice.

## The 10 Styles

### 1. Transactional Leadership
- Definition: Leaders give instructions and use rewards/penalties based on task completion.
- Pro: Confusion and guesswork are eliminated.
- Con: Creativity and innovation may be stifled due to rigid expectations.
- Best used: Clear direction required; team motivated by explicit directions and rewards.

### 2. Transformational Leadership
- Definition: Leaders inspire employees to innovate by empowering them to suggest improvements.
- Pro: Establish a high level of trust with employees.
- Con: Desire for change can create friction in process-focused environments.
- Best used: Need buy-in for organizational shifts; soliciting improvement ideas.

### 3. Servant Leadership
- Definition: Leaders prioritize others' needs above their own, focusing on elevating team members.
- Pro: Boosts morale, builds trust, improves performance and company culture.
- Con: Challenging to consistently deprioritize personal needs.
- Best used: Building trust with new teams; supporting teams through challenges.

### 4. Democratic Leadership
- Definition: Participative approach emphasizing collaboration and active team involvement in decision-making.
- Pro: Creativity and innovation are encouraged.
- Con: Consensus-building can be inefficient and costly.
- Best used: Adequate time for decisions; when others have relevant expertise.

### 5. Autocratic Leadership
- Definition: "My way or the highway" approach where leaders make decisions and dictate how tasks are accomplished.
- Pro: Decisions made quickly and strategically.
- Con: Employees may feel ignored, restricted, or abused.
- Best used: Complex crises; urgent situations requiring speedy decisions.

### 6. Bureaucratic Leadership
- Definition: Leaders follow prescribed rules and systems; authority derives from formal position rather than personal traits.
- Pro: Provides stability through a systematized approach.
- Con: Can be inflexible and ignore employee creativity.
- Best used: Strict compliance requirements; routine, predictable processes.

### 7. Laissez-Faire Leadership
- Definition: Hands-off approach providing tools/resources but allowing teams to make decisions independently.
- Pro: Empowering for creative, self-motivated teams.
- Con: Chaos and confusion possible with unorganized teams.
- Best used: Expert, accountable, organized teams; fostering autonomy.

### 8. Charismatic Leadership
- Definition: Leaders use magnetic personalities, eloquent communication, and persuasion to unite teams around shared objectives.
- Pro: Inspirational and effective at team investment in goals.
- Con: Risk of tunnel vision, losing sight of other important issues.
- Best used: Acting as "face" of an initiative; teams dealing with uncertainty.

### 9. Coaching Leadership
- Definition: Leaders act as coaches offering guidance and mentorship; invested in others' development.
- Pro: Builds trust, which can improve employee satisfaction, motivation, retention.
- Con: Time and effort-intensive, especially for large teams.
- Best used: Organizations with leadership skepticism; low engagement despite skill presence.

### 10. Situational Leadership
- Definition: Highly flexible approach adapting to specific circumstances rather than using blanket methods.
- Pro: Delivers what situations/teams require in specific moments.
- Con: Requires significant situational awareness and emotional intelligence.
- Best used: Constantly changing teams; diverse personalities with different preferences.
