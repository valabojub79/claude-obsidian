# Leadership Styles (Simply Psychology)

- Author: Charlotte Nickerson; Reviewer: Saul McLeod, PhD; Associate Editor: Olivia Guy-Evans, MSc
- Publication: Simply Psychology

## Main thesis

Lewin's 1939 foundational research on leadership styles provides the basis for modern leadership theories; researchers have since identified additional styles beyond his original framework.

## Lewin's original three styles (1939)

1. Autocratic - leaders make decisions independently without group input; clear division between leader and members. Fast decisions, reduces stress under pressure. Drawbacks: fear/frustration, reduced communication, less creative decisions.
2. Democratic - leaders offer guidance while encouraging participation; shared responsibility. Higher-quality contributions, higher morale. Drawbacks: slower decisions, over-dependence.
3. Laissez-Faire - team autonomy to self-direct; leader sets vision, subordinates determine implementation. Builds responsibility, fast problem-solving. Requires highly skilled members.

## Additional styles

- Transformational - models behavior, sets clear goals/high expectations, vision/values centered; culture avoids blame. Disadvantage: ineffective before trust is built; de-emphasizes operational details.
- Transactional - leader-follower relationship as transaction; compensation for meeting criteria. Kahai et al. (2004): group efficacy higher under transactional condition.
- Strategic - balances influencing teams toward long-term success with current context; links vision to daily work.
- Bureaucratic - clear hierarchies, strict regulations, conformity; scalable, predictable; limits advancement, inflexible.
- Coach-style - collaboration and guidance via individual strengths/weaknesses; Berg & Karlsen (2016): replaces short-term problem-solving with longer-term strategic thinking. Example: Satya Nadella at Microsoft shifted culture from "knowing" to learning.
- Participative - all members encouraged to provide input on goals and decisions.

- Davies & Davies (2004); Kahai, Sosik & Avolio (2004); MacKenzie, Podsakoff & Rich (2001); Berg & Karlsen (2016); Lee et al. (2020); Chaudhry & Javed (2012).

## Key finding

MacKenzie et al. (2001): transformational leader behaviors have a stronger direct and indirect relationship with sales performance than transactional approaches.
